What Claimwell is

Claimwell is a free, independent guide to class action settlements. It helps you find settlements you may qualify for, get ready for the official claim form, and keep track of claims you file.

  • Claimwell is not a law firm, and nothing in it is legal advice. Using Claimwell doesn't create an attorney-client relationship. If you have questions about your legal rights, talk to a lawyer, or to the class counsel listed in the settlement notice, who represent class members at no cost to them.
  • Claimwell is not affiliated with any court, settlement administrator, law firm or company named in it. Company and product names are used only to identify settlements.
  • A settlement isn't a court finding that the company did anything wrong. Claimwell describes what each case is about; it doesn't say anyone broke the law.

Eligibility and payments are decided by others

Claimwell's eligibility results are a guide based on your answers and on the settlement notices. The court-appointed settlement administrator decides whether a claim is valid and how much it pays. Claimwell can't promise that you qualify, that a claim will be approved, or that you'll be paid any amount. Payout amounts shown in Claimwell come from the notices and are often estimates that change with the number of claims.

Accuracy

We work to keep settlement information accurate, and each settlement shows when it was last checked and where the information came from. But deadlines, requirements and amounts can change, and information can be incomplete or wrong. The official settlement website and the court's notice are the final word. Check them before you file and before any deadline.

Your responsibilities

  • Only file claims you're eligible for, and only tell the truth. Claim forms are usually signed under penalty of perjury. False claims can be rejected and can have legal consequences.
  • You file every claim yourself. Claimwell never submits a claim for you. When you use Fill my details, check every field before you submit.
  • Filing usually affects your rights. Staying in a settlement typically means giving up the right to sue the company separately over the same issue. The notice explains your options, including excluding yourself or objecting.
  • Keep your device secure. Information you enter is stored on it.
