Who's included

  • People and businesses who owned or leased certain Hyundai or Kia cars at any time up to April 14, 2025.
  • Models: 2011–2019 Hyundai Sonata and Sonata Hybrid, 2018–2023 Kona, 2022–2023 Kona N, 2019–2021 Veloster, 2010–2013 Kia Forte and Forte Koup, 2011–2020 Optima, 2011–2016 Optima Hybrid, 2011–2012 and 2014 Sedona.
  • The car must have first been sold or leased in the U.S. or a U.S. territory; eligibility is confirmed by VIN.
  • The lawsuit says the cars' ZF-TRW airbag control units may fail in a crash; Hyundai and Kia deny it.

The official notice has the exact definition. If you're not sure, JND Legal Administration can tell you before the deadline.

How much you could get

Up to $350 (recalled) or $150

Up to $350 for recalled vehicles or $150 for unrecalled ones, plus reimbursement of documented recall costs for recalled vehicles. Maximums; actual amounts depend on claims.

What you'll need

  • VIN for each vehicle.
  • Unique ID from your notice, or title/registration showing your name and VIN.
  • Receipts for rental, towing or repair costs, if any.

Proof required. Claims are tied to your VIN. With the Unique ID from a notice, nothing else is needed; without one, title, registration or lease papers showing your name and VIN.
